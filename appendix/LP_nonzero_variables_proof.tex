
\begin{lemma}[Nonzero Variables in the LP Solution]
\label{lem:lp_nonzero_variables}
For the LP relaxation of \Cref{eq:bp-ilp}, there exists
an optimal vertex solution $\mathbf{a}^*$ with at most
$m$ nonzero variables.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The feasible region of the LP relaxation is
\[
P = \{\mathbf{a} \geq \mathbf{0} :
A\mathbf{a} \geq \mathbf{n}\},
\]
where $A$ is the configuration matrix with $m$ rows,
corresponding to the $m$ distinct item types.

Since the LP has a finite optimum and its feasible
region contains no lines, there exists an optimal
solution $\mathbf{a}^*$ that is a vertex of $P$.

Suppose, for contradiction, that $\mathbf{a}^*$ has
$k > m$ positive components. Let $S$ denote the
indices of these components, so that $|S|=k$.

The corresponding $k$ columns of $A$ are vectors
in $\mathbb{R}^m$. Since $k>m$, these columns
must be linearly dependent. Therefore, there exists
a nonzero vector $\mathbf{d}$ such that
\[
A\mathbf{d}=\mathbf{0},
\qquad
d_j=0 \quad \text{for all } j\notin S.
\]

Since $a_j^*>0$ for every $j\in S$, we can choose
a sufficiently small $\epsilon>0$ such that
\[
\mathbf{a}^*+\epsilon\mathbf{d}\geq\mathbf{0},
\qquad
\mathbf{a}^*-\epsilon\mathbf{d}\geq\mathbf{0}.
\]

Moreover, since $A\mathbf{d}=\mathbf{0}$,
\[
A(\mathbf{a}^*\pm\epsilon\mathbf{d})
=A\mathbf{a}^*
\geq\mathbf{n}.
\]
Thus, both $\mathbf{a}^*+\epsilon\mathbf{d}$
and $\mathbf{a}^*-\epsilon\mathbf{d}$ are feasible.

However,
\[
\mathbf{a}^*
=
\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{a}^*+\epsilon\mathbf{d})
+
\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{a}^*-\epsilon\mathbf{d}).
\]
Since $\mathbf{d}\neq\mathbf{0}$, these are two
distinct feasible solutions. This contradicts
the assumption that $\mathbf{a}^*$ is a vertex
of the feasible region.

Therefore, $\mathbf{a}^*$ has at most $m$
nonzero components.
\end{proof}
